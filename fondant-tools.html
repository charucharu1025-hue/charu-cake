    Fondant 101

    The basics every new fondant baker should know.

    How thick to roll

    Covering a cake3 to 5 mm
    Ribbons and bows2 to 3 mm
    Flowers and petals1 to 2 mm

    Roll on a lightly dusted surface, and turn the fondant often so it does not stick.

    What each tool does

    Plunger cutterCuts and presses petals
    Modelling toolShapes and softens edges
    SmootherGives a flat, even finish
    Wheel or ribbon cutterCuts strips and edges
    Silicone mouldCopies fine detail fast

    Handy tips

    • Dust your surface and tools lightly with cornflour or icing sugar.
    • Keep unused fondant tightly wrapped so it does not dry out.
    • Use a tiny dab of edible glue or water to stick pieces together.
    • Wash new tools in warm soapy water and dry them before first use.

    Fondant questions, answered

    Do I need all of these tools?

    No. A rolling pin, a smoother and a few plunger cutters will take you a long way. The complete kit is a good start, and you can add moulds and mats later.

    How do I stop fondant sticking?

    Lightly dust your surface, rolling pin and tools with cornflour or icing sugar. Dust moulds and mats too, and tap off the extra.

    What should I use to stick fondant pieces together?

    A tiny amount of water works for small pieces. For heavier decorations, use edible glue.

    Can I put fondant decorations on a buttercream cake?

    Yes. Make them ahead and let them firm up, then place them close to serving time, because moisture can soften them.
